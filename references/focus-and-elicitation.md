# Focus and participant meaning

**Method baseline 1.1 · provisional. Evidence baseline: 2026-10-23, approved specification Revision 6.** These are evidence-informed behavioral contracts, not validated interventions or assessment instruments. IDs remain stable across revisions; preserve the version and evidence baseline with any rubric or evaluation using them. A source revision requires explicit review, not a silent change to a frozen evaluation.

## Purpose: an evolving understanding of the person and relationship

PREPARE and DIGEST serve a deeper, revisable evidence-grounded model of this person in this relationship: values and priorities, decision criteria, communication patterns, supported motivations and tensions, competing explanations, and consequential gaps. Use references/person-model.md for the model structure and safeguards ([person model](person-model.md)). “Digital twin” is an aspiration to useful contextual understanding, not literal replication or validated psychology. 

## Asynchronous use: PREPARE and DIGEST

**Product orientation 0.2.0: prepare the human before a conversation; digest notes or, preferably, a transcript afterward.** Adaptation happens across conversations. The agent does not monitor, manage or steer the live exchange. Conversational moves below are optional approaches for the human to consider, never instructions the agent executes on the other person.

In **PREPARE**, recall the authorized person/relationship model and identify a consequential uncertainty between competing hypotheses. Offer a small selection of personalized investigative approaches with reasons and a private account of what their possible answers could teach. A possible question or reflection must fit the history, vocabulary and purpose; avoid a script, required sequence or quota. The human chooses whether and how to use it, including taking practical action or making no inquiry.

In **DIGEST**, inspect what the supplied transcript or notes actually establish. Preparation suggestions are not evidence that a question was asked, an answer occurred, or agreement formed. Prefer a transcript for wording and sequence; notes may be selective recollections. Attribute uncertain speakers tentatively, preserve missing context and distinguish quoted words from paraphrase. Do not invent responses, motives, pauses or nonverbal behavior. Check interpretations against corrections and contrary evidence, then enrich, narrow or overturn the person/relationship hypotheses, revise the next preparation and propose any authorized memory change. A summary alone is not this model update. There is no presumed live feedback channel.

Load when the current purpose is unclear or a consequential expression needs clarification. Begin with the actual history: what the person has already said, what matters to the shared task, and what remains uncertain. A new encounter does not require an interview, and an established agenda does not need repeated permission checks.

## Choosing an adaptive move

Identify the smallest uncertainty whose resolution could change useful action. Check whether it was already answered, whether the wording or transcript is reliable, and whether the answer is appropriate to request. Then choose a natural question, tentative paraphrase, summary, practical action, or no inquiry. Form language from the preceding contribution and shared context rather than selecting a stock probe. Thoughtfully designed curiosity is sincere when possible answers can change understanding, even when the human also hopes to develop a collaboration or professional opportunity.

Use the speaker's terminology when it carries their intended meaning. Avoid presupposing an emotion, motive, problem or desired outcome. A metaphor belongs to its speaker: ask about its relevant meaning only when needed, without decoding symbols or extending the metaphor for them. One useful clarification can settle the issue; an intriguing phrase does not authorize deeper personal exploration.

Before a follow-up, ask internally what the last answer changed. If nothing changed and the task can proceed, stop. A short or mixed answer can be sufficient. Neither questions per minute nor amount of disclosure measures listening, truth or relationship quality. No script bank, required sequence, fixed question quota or personality rubric follows from these contracts.

## Prepare an inquiry that could change understanding

Start with competing explanations grounded in actual history, not a generic personality dimension. Identify why distinguishing them matters to the relationship or a real decision. A private preparation note should explain what the optional approach could teach, what evidence currently supports each explanation, and what would weaken the favored one. Keep that reasoning distinct from language the human might naturally use; the human need not recite the analyst’s hypotheses to the other person.

Anticipate plausible answer interpretations and their limits. An answer can favor one explanation, support several, expose an overlooked explanation or remain uninformative. Describe those branches qualitatively, with optional follow-ups only if a new relevant uncertainty arises; a decline or adequate answer ends exploration. Do not assign answer-to-trait mappings, readiness scores or fabricated probabilities. The person may change the framing entirely. A useful preparation makes the human more curious and less committed to a preferred story.

For example, actual prior evidence might show enthusiasm for a pilot followed by rejection of a larger rollout. Competing explanations could be concern about reversible commitments, an implementation burden, or a newly changed priority. A relevant approach would explore what differed between those decisions using the person’s own account. Workload evidence could strengthen the burden explanation; rejection of even a low-burden reversible option could weaken it without proving another explanation. This is an investigative principle, not a stock question: use it only where this relationship’s evidence supplies that contrast. Do not seek private reasons merely because they would make the model richer.

DIGEST checks the actual record against the alternatives, including counterevidence and ambiguity. If the human never explored the contrast, record the gap rather than treat a prepared branch as an observation. New evidence may add a decision criterion, narrow a claim to one context, reveal a tension or overturn the earlier account; carry that change into the next preparation.

## MC-01: Establish a shared focus

**Method baseline 1.1 · provisional; evidence baseline 2026-10-23.**

- **Uncertainty addressed:** What would make this conversation useful, and which topics are welcome now?
- **Context:** New contact, changed agenda, multiple possible purposes or evidence of mismatch.
- **Evidence needed:** Current stated purpose, relationship context, time constraints and previous boundaries. Silence alone does not establish the current purpose.
- **Possible moves:** PREPARE: recommend an optional agenda check, relevant topic choice or concise purpose summary; if the shared task is clear, recommend proceeding. DIGEST: identify the actual stated purpose, redirections and boundaries from the supplied record.
- **Alternatives:** Apparent disengagement may reflect time pressure, a wrong agenda or no current need, not a personality characteristic.
- **Disconfirmation:** A recorded redirection, correction or decline defeats the proposed focus; revise the next preparation. Missing evidence of agreement is not agreement.
- **Stopping:** A settled agenda needs no repeated checking; a decline or lack of relevance ends recommended inquiry. DIGEST stops at the evidence supplied rather than filling missing discussion.
- **Source depth:** [INT-01](evidence-index.md#int-01) and [MI-02](evidence-index.md#mi-02) full methodological/professional guidance; no claim this contract alone improves sales.

## MC-02: Elicit meaning without importing an answer

**Method baseline 1.1 · provisional; evidence baseline 2026-10-23.**

- **Uncertainty addressed:** What does the person mean by a relevant expression, account or metaphor?
- **Context:** Ambiguous terminology or a consequential gap; use the person’s own vocabulary.
- **Evidence needed:** Exact preceding language and context; distinguish transcript error from unclear meaning.
- **Possible moves:** PREPARE: suggest an optional open clarification of a consequential expression using prior wording, or no inquiry where already answered. DIGEST: inspect the actual elaboration and context; do not treat the suggested question as asked or a recalled paraphrase as an exact quote.
- **Alternatives:** Metaphor may be casual, contextual or borrowed; it does not encode a universal trait.
- **Disconfirmation:** The actual account rejects the interpretation, supplies another meaning or makes the topic irrelevant. Revise on that evidence; an unasked preparation question yields no answer.
- **Stopping:** Sufficient meaning, intrusiveness or a recorded decline ends recommended exploration. Missing evidence stays unresolved; no automatic outreach or live follow-up.
- **Source depth:** [CL-02](evidence-index.md#cl-02) full rating-method manuscript; [INT-01](evidence-index.md#int-01) full methodology; [ASK-01](evidence-index.md#ask-01) full experiments with correction/debate. No independent Clean Language superiority demonstrated.

## Applying correction

When the digest records a redirection, revise the next preparation toward that relevant focus or leave it alone. If a clarified term defeats the original interpretation, withdraw that interpretation and change the next preparation. Do not preserve a hidden preferred answer. Record the relevant account with attribution, scope and uncertainty.
