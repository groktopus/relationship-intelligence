# Consumer-depth methods for person-model learning

Load when the consequential gap concerns why a particular feature, option or experience matters to this person, or when direct questions produce abstract, thin or difficult-to-interpret accounts. These methods supply optional ways to learn across PREPARE and DIGEST. They are not a required interview sequence. Use the [person model](person-model.md) to retain competing explanations, contextual confidence and implications.

**Method revision 0.1 · research access 2026-10-23.** The operational adaptations below are proposed skill behavior. Foundational method guidance and a recent projective-data illustration do not establish that this combination accurately recovers enduring motives or improves relationships. Source access depth and empirical limits appear below.

## Begin with an episode and a consequential distinction

Recall the person's actual choices, language, corrections and prior answers before choosing a method. Identify a difference whose explanation would change the next conversation: why one option was useful while another was rejected; why an attribute matters in one setting; or what consequence makes a tradeoff acceptable. Name at least one alternative explanation before proposing inquiry.

Prepare one or a few natural moves, not a questionnaire. Privately explain the learning aim, plausible interpretations, what would count against the leading account and how the result could change action. Use the person's words; avoid introducing an emotion or a value as if already established. A concrete recollection, contrast, tentative reflection or practical option can reveal more than another abstract question. The qualitative-interview guidance in [INT-01](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3933509/) supports flexible, response-contingent exploration, while its patient-reported-outcome context limits direct transfer to ordinary relationship work.

DIGEST works from actual notes or preferably transcript spans. Preserve the prompt as well as the answer: a reason volunteered before prompting has a different elicitation history from agreement with the human's suggested reason. Separate speech, paraphrase, interviewer interpretation and model interpretation. Prepared questions never become observed events unless the supplied record establishes their use.

## Means-end laddering: feature, consequence, significance

**Uncertainty:** What makes a stated attribute or preferred option consequential for this person in the relevant situation?

The means-end distinction connects an attribute of a product or option to its consequences and to personally significant ends. The inspected [Reynolds and Gutman article excerpt](https://docsbay.net/doc/482839/laddering-theory-method-analysis-and-interpretation) describes a tailored one-to-one technique and distinguishes elicitation from subsequent analysis. Adapt the reasoning, not repeated wording or a requirement to reach a deepest value.

**PREPARE:** Start from an actual meaningful distinction. If someone preferred a bounded test to a larger rollout, explore what the boundedness changes for them before guessing that it means caution, control or independence. Privately distinguish several candidate links: reduced workload, better evidence, easier reversal, customer impact or doubts about this proposal. A natural move could revisit what made that smaller test useful on that occasion. If they name a consequence, the optional next move explores why that consequence mattered there. If the concrete consequence answers the practical question, stop without extracting an abstract value.

Keep possible levels separate:

| Elicited element | What it supports | What remains open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feature: a two-week trial | A named option attribute | Why the duration matters |
| Consequence: less disruption during delivery | Their stated consequence in context | General workload preference versus current capacity |
| Significance: keeping promises to existing customers | Their account of what mattered | How consistently this criterion outranks alternatives |

This synthetic chain is illustrative, not a coding answer key. Do not add an endpoint the speaker did not supply. Different branches may coexist, and their links can be tentative or absent.

**DIGEST:** Retain each supported link with the actual span, event context and elicitation history. Distinguish “speaker linked X to Y” from “agent infers X causes Y.” Mark missing links unknown. One prompted chain is one source occasion, not three independent confirmations. Compare contrary episodes: if a larger rollout was accepted under spare capacity, that weakens a global small-test preference and supports a capacity explanation. If workload is unchanged but the choice changes with reversibility, revise the leading criterion accordingly.

**Disconfirmation/stopping:** A rejected paraphrase, different consequence, weak speaker attribution or an already sufficient answer can defeat or end the branch. Repeated abstract probes may produce a plausible story merely because an answer is expected. Stop when useful meaning is clear or no further discriminating evidence is available. A chain can describe an account without proving an enduring motivation.

## Depth interviewing: sequence, contrast and counterexample

**Uncertainty:** Is an explanation grounded in what happened, or does it compress several different situations into a convenient generalization?

**PREPARE:** Choose a relevant episode and invite its sequence: what was happening, which options were considered, what changed the choice and what followed. Then use only a consequential contrast—another occasion, an exception or what would differ under changed conditions. Ask for a concrete example before interpreting broad terms such as “flexibility” or “good support.” Keep the human's practical purpose visible; do not turn ordinary preparation into an exhaustive interview.

Private interpretation branches should anticipate several possibilities. A detailed account may support a decision criterion; a vague account may reflect recall limits, unfamiliar language or low relevance. It does not establish evasiveness. A counterexample can narrow a pattern without erasing all prior evidence. The distinction might change proposal scope, information needed, timing or follow-through.

**DIGEST:** Reconstruct only the sequence the record supports. Separate what the person says they sought, what they report doing and the outcome. Do not assume an explanation was operative at the time rather than constructed retrospectively. Compare episodes and retain their context. Revise a model when a new distinction changes it; retain uncertainty when the exchange adds only another version of the same account.

**Disconfirmation/stopping:** A corrected sequence, missing alternative, contrary example or unclear date weakens a simple explanation. When the account already settles the practical issue, proceed to the useful action rather than manufacturing another probe.

## Projective and hypothetical elicitation: explore associations without transferring identity

**Uncertainty:** Which meanings, associations or possible tradeoffs are easier to discuss through an example than a direct abstract self-description?

Projective tasks can use ambiguous stimuli, sentence completion, personification or imagined situations. A [2026 publisher article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41270-026-00540-9) combines a review with primary destination-image data. It illustrates that frequency-level associations and contextual interpretations can complement each other; it does not validate inference from an imagined character to the respondent's enduring motives.

**PREPARE:** Use a hypothetical only where it illuminates the current uncertainty. State the imagined conditions clearly and vary one meaningful feature if a comparison is useful. An optional move might explore how a comparable team would weigh a reversible trial during a delivery crunch. Privately identify what competing predictions distinguish: capacity, customer consequences, evidence quality or proposal-specific doubt. An answer could expose a possibility the human overlooked without describing the speaker's own preference.

A naturally arising metaphor or association can be clarified in its speaker's meaning. Do not decode a symbol through a universal interpretation dictionary. If someone characterizes an imagined buyer as “careful,” explore what that means in the story before treating it as a decision criterion.

**DIGEST:** Keep target and speaker separate. “The speaker thinks this fictional manager would reject the trial” is an attributed third-party judgment. It could reflect a stereotype, a norm, a known colleague, the wording of the vignette or personal experience; it is not automatically the speaker's own trait, intention or behavior. Preserve hypothetical modality. Only an actual self-link, relevant episode or independent behavior can support moving the interpretation toward a person-specific hypothesis, and even then scope the claim to that evidence.

**Disconfirmation/stopping:** Ask what in the situation produced the judgment if the distinction matters. If changing one vignette condition changes the answer, narrow the explanation to that condition. If the person rejects the analogy or no real-world comparison exists, retain an association rather than an inferred motive. Imagined acceptance is not a commitment.

## Elicitation effects and analytic reliability

The interview contributes to the answer. Reynolds and Gutman's inspected sections explicitly warn about demand characteristics and approval/disapproval cues. PREPARE should therefore avoid rewarding one interpretation, repeatedly reformulating toward a preferred answer, or presenting competing labels as exhaustive. DIGEST should retain leading premises, offered alternatives, order and any visible correction. Do not invent nonverbal cues absent from the record. A polished explanation after repeated prompting may be co-constructed rather than independently volunteered.

For model maintenance, keep source spans, speaker/context, elicited distinctions, possible codes and the reason for the final interpretation. Allow multiple codes, uncoded material and unresolved disagreement. Preserve negative cases and valence: similar words can convey endorsement, worry, irony or a hypothetical voice. An aggregate map or common code is not an individual person's authoritative explanation.

When a formal evaluation uses coded responses, define the unit and category meanings before comparing coders, use genuinely independent coding on the same units where appropriate, and retain disagreements before reconciliation. The statistical reliability procedure must fit the design and available data; do not report a coefficient never measured. Repeated answers from the same model or several labels on one span are not independent coder/source evidence. Coding agreement concerns consistency of interpretation under a coding procedure, not truth of the inferred motive. For an ordinary single-agent digest, disclose qualitative interpretation rather than implying measured inter-coder reliability.

## Evidence depth and current limits

- **Reynolds & Gutman, 1988:** original method exposition, accessed as a partial mirrored article transcription ending during elicitation instructions. Supports the attributes–consequences–values distinction and inspected interviewer cautions; full analysis/results and source-copy fidelity remain unverified. No efficacy estimate is used.
- **[Grunert & Grunert, 1995](https://pure.au.dk/portal/en/publications/measuring-subjective-meaning-structures-by-the-laddering-method-t/):** university abstract only. Reports validity concerns across elicitation, coding and analysis, illustrated with laddering-study material and respondent/interviewer accounts. No specific uninspected criteria or numerical results are imported.
- **[France, Albinsson & Luintel, 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41270-026-00540-9):** substantial publisher article text inspected, including original study with 173 undergraduate business students and destination-image prompts; supplements not inspected. Same-data qualitative/quantitative convergence and richer valence interpretation are empirical illustrations, not evidence of individual psychological accuracy or causal relationship improvement.
- **[Qualitative interviewing guidance, INT-01](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3933509/):** substantive method text inspected; guidance/review for patient-reported-outcome development, not a consumer or relationship efficacy trial. It supports interviewing mechanics within that transfer limit.

The PREPARE/DIGEST procedures here are a synthesis for testing. Retain access limits, alternatives and failed interpretations when revising them. Method use should deepen a corrigible explanation and change a useful decision, rather than produce stronger labels from the same thin evidence.
