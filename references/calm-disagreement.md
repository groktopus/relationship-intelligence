# Calm listening in ordinary disagreement

**Method baseline 1.1 · provisional. Evidence baseline: 2026-10-23, approved specification Revision 6.** These are evidence-informed behavioral contracts, not validated interventions or assessment instruments. IDs remain stable across revisions; preserve the version and evidence baseline with any rubric or evaluation using them. A source revision requires explicit review, not a silent change to a frozen evaluation.

## Purpose: an evolving understanding of the person and relationship

PREPARE and DIGEST serve a deeper, revisable evidence-grounded model of this person in this relationship: values and priorities, decision criteria, communication patterns, supported motivations and tensions, competing explanations, and consequential gaps. Use references/person-model.md for the model structure and safeguards ([person model](person-model.md)). “Digital twin” is an aspiration to useful contextual understanding, not literal replication or validated psychology. 

## Asynchronous use: PREPARE and DIGEST

**Product orientation 0.2.0: prepare the human before a conversation; digest notes or, preferably, a transcript afterward.** Adaptation happens across conversations. The agent does not monitor, manage or steer the live exchange. Conversational moves below are optional approaches for the human to consider, never instructions the agent executes on the other person.

In **PREPARE**, recall the authorized person/relationship model and identify a consequential uncertainty between competing hypotheses. Offer a small selection of personalized investigative approaches with reasons and a private account of what their possible answers could teach. A possible question or reflection must fit the history, vocabulary and purpose; avoid a script, required sequence or quota. The human chooses whether and how to use it, including taking practical action or making no inquiry.

In **DIGEST**, inspect what the supplied transcript or notes actually establish. Preparation suggestions are not evidence that a question was asked, an answer occurred, or agreement formed. Prefer a transcript for wording and sequence; notes may be selective recollections. Attribute uncertain speakers tentatively, preserve missing context and distinguish quoted words from paraphrase. Do not invent responses, motives, pauses or nonverbal behavior. Check interpretations against corrections and contrary evidence, then enrich, narrow or overturn the person/relationship hypotheses, revise the next preparation and propose any authorized memory change. A summary alone is not this model update. There is no presumed live feedback channel.

Load when tension or misunderstanding obstructs an ordinary discussion in which participation is free. This reference supplies no crisis-response capability and no objective of controlling a person. This reference applies to ordinary, freely participated disagreement; crisis operations require different expertise.

## Respond to the expressed concern

Establish what was actually said and what decision is blocked. Anger may reflect a justified concern or violated boundary. Slowing the pace, a concise tentative reflection, a summary of the disagreement, space to think, a break or ending the discussion can each be appropriate. Choose according to the contribution and available context; do not perform a standard sequence on someone.

Keep an emotional interpretation tentative and supported by expressed words. Acknowledge a substantive problem or correct an error where warranted. A soothing tone without addressing the problem may feel dismissive. Do not demand calmness as a condition for considering a legitimate concern, promise compliance, or read composure as agreement, trust or truth.

If the actual record shows a reflection misstated the concern or increased tension, withdraw the added assumption in DIGEST and revise the next preparation. Repeatedly renaming an emotion after correction is not listening. If the person asks for a break or ends the discussion, respect that boundary. Silence must not be used to pressure further disclosure.

## Learn from disagreement at the supported scope

When the actual record identifies a concrete unmet expectation, decision criterion or relationship tension, DIGEST may revise that part of the model. Compare situational explanations before inferring an enduring communication pattern. PREPARE can propose an optional approach to distinguish a disputed factual premise from a difference in priorities, with reasons and possible counterevidence. Interpret anger, avoidance or calmness through the recorded concern and context before considering a broader pattern. Sometimes the sound model update is a clearer boundary and no further inquiry.

## MC-10: Use calm listening without importing crisis control

**Method baseline 1.1 · provisional; evidence baseline 2026-10-23.**

- **Uncertainty addressed:** Whether escalation or misunderstanding is obstructing ordinary discussion.
- **Context:** Non-emergency disagreement where both parties can freely participate; not a crisis-response capability.
- **Evidence needed:** Expressed concerns and interaction context, without inferring mental state from a single cue.
- **Possible moves:** PREPARE: suggest optional ways for the human to address an expressed concern, summarize, slow the pace, offer a break or end an ordinary discussion, without promising compliance. DIGEST: inspect recorded concerns, corrections and outcomes; do not infer tension, emotion or success from missing cues.
- **Alternatives:** Anger or refusal can be justified; calm presentation does not mean agreement, trust or truth.
- **Disconfirmation:** The supplied record shows that a listening approach felt patronizing, misstated the concern or increased tension. Revise future advice; absence of such feedback does not prove success.
- **Stopping:** A break request, ended discussion or context outside ordinary disagreement ends this recommended approach. The agent interprets the supplied record afterward.
- **Source depth:** [CRISIS-01](evidence-index.md#crisis-01)/02 full FBI practitioner guidance, explicitly distinct from empirical validation; [LISTEN-01](evidence-index.md#listen-01) full experiment has narrower ordinary-interaction outcomes. Police authority and crisis-control objectives do not transfer.

## Scope of borrowed guidance

CRISIS-01/02 are full FBI practitioner articles describing a repertoire and training framework, not controlled evidence for ordinary collaboration. Police authority, crisis-control objectives, training scores and compliance goals do not transfer. LISTEN-01 addresses narrower outcomes in brief initial interactions. No source here validates lie detection, a psychological score, or a reliable escalation-management intervention by an agent. Direct sources and limits: references/evidence-index.md.
