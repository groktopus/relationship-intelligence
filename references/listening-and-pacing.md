# Listening, summaries and space

**Method baseline 1.1 · provisional. Evidence baseline: 2026-10-23, approved specification Revision 6.** These are evidence-informed behavioral contracts, not validated interventions or assessment instruments. IDs remain stable across revisions; preserve the version and evidence baseline with any rubric or evaluation using them. A source revision requires explicit review, not a silent change to a frozen evaluation.

## Purpose: an evolving understanding of the person and relationship

PREPARE and DIGEST serve a deeper, revisable evidence-grounded model of this person in this relationship: values and priorities, decision criteria, communication patterns, supported motivations and tensions, competing explanations, and consequential gaps. Use references/person-model.md for the model structure and safeguards ([person model](person-model.md)). “Digital twin” is an aspiration to useful contextual understanding, not literal replication or validated psychology. 

## Asynchronous use: PREPARE and DIGEST

**Product orientation 0.2.0: prepare the human before a conversation; digest notes or, preferably, a transcript afterward.** Adaptation happens across conversations. The agent does not monitor, manage or steer the live exchange. Conversational moves below are optional approaches for the human to consider, never instructions the agent executes on the other person.

In **PREPARE**, recall the authorized person/relationship model and identify a consequential uncertainty between competing hypotheses. Offer a small selection of personalized investigative approaches with reasons and a private account of what their possible answers could teach. A possible question or reflection must fit the history, vocabulary and purpose; avoid a script, required sequence or quota. The human chooses whether and how to use it, including taking practical action or making no inquiry.

In **DIGEST**, inspect what the supplied transcript or notes actually establish. Preparation suggestions are not evidence that a question was asked, an answer occurred, or agreement formed. Prefer a transcript for wording and sequence; notes may be selective recollections. Attribute uncertain speakers tentatively, preserve missing context and distinguish quoted words from paraphrase. Do not invent responses, motives, pauses or nonverbal behavior. Check interpretations against corrections and contrary evidence, then enrich, narrow or overturn the person/relationship hypotheses, revise the next preparation and propose any authorized memory change. A summary alone is not this model update. There is no presumed live feedback channel.

Load for uncertain understanding, multiple concerns, a transition, or the decision whether more inquiry is welcome. Listening is responsive attention to the actual contribution, including requests for advice, action, a pause or an end. It is not a requirement to reflect every statement.

## Choosing reflection, summary or space

A reflection is a tentative meaning hypothesis. Keep it close to expressed words, concise enough to correct, and free of invented hidden motives. Reflect an emotion only where the person's account supports it, and leave room for another meaning. Acknowledgment can suffice when the statement is already clear. When the supplied history shows a practical request, recommend a relevant practical answer for the human; reflective exploration must not become a barrier to help.

A summary collects established meaning without converting claims into facts or disagreement into consensus. Preserve important reservations, uncertainty, speaker attribution and decisions still open. Check emphasis where it could change a consequential decision. A polished summary that omits reasons against action is a distortion, even if every included sentence is accurate.

A pause can provide thinking space. Do not infer why silence occurred, time it as an extraction technique, or fill it automatically. A delay in text may be technical or ordinary turn-taking. A boundary or adequate answer can mean no further inquiry at all. Defer, return to the task, or stop according to context; no universal pause duration is prescribed.

## What listening evidence contributes to the model

A recorded clarification may distinguish a decision criterion from a passing preference, or reveal that the agent’s account of a concern was wrong. DIGEST uses that correction to revise hypotheses and next preparation. A summary should preserve conflicting priorities and unresolved alternatives rather than merely compress the meeting. A recorded request for directness or space may support a scoped communication preference in this relationship; one pause or terse note does not establish it. Model depth never creates a duty to disclose more.

## MC-03: Reflect and check a tentative understanding

**Method baseline 1.1 · provisional; evidence baseline 2026-10-23.**

- **Uncertainty addressed:** Whether the listener has understood the person’s meaning or concern.
- **Context:** Ambiguity, an important concern or risk of premature advice.
- **Evidence needed:** Actual words and context. An emotion interpretation needs support and remains tentative.
- **Possible moves:** PREPARE: suggest a concise tentative paraphrase or acknowledgment if understanding is uncertain, with a reason; an explicit practical request may call for action instead. DIGEST: check what was actually expressed and whether any recorded correction supports or defeats the interpretation.
- **Alternatives:** They may want practical advice or action rather than reflective exploration; ask or use an explicit request.
- **Disconfirmation:** Recorded correction, irritation or mismatch, or the agent’s recognition of an added assumption, defeats the reflection. Withdraw it in the digest and revise future preparation; notes may not establish the reaction.
- **Stopping:** Adequate understanding or evidence that reflection is repetitive/unwelcome ends that recommendation. Do not fabricate feedback when the record lacks it.
- **Source depth:** [LISTEN-01](evidence-index.md#listen-01) full randomized experiment supports reported understanding in a specific lab context; [MI-02](evidence-index.md#mi-02) full guidance supplies hypothesis-checking. Satisfaction/attraction differences versus advice were not statistically significant in [LISTEN-01](evidence-index.md#listen-01); this is not proof of equivalence.

## MC-04: Summarize and preserve disagreement

**Method baseline 1.1 · provisional; evidence baseline 2026-10-23.**

- **Uncertainty addressed:** Which priorities, concerns and unresolved issues the conversation has established.
- **Context:** Several topics, a transition, a proposed decision or conflicting accounts.
- **Evidence needed:** Relevant statements with speaker/time/source; distinguish shared agreement from one party’s claim.
- **Possible moves:** PREPARE: offer a brief human-facing summary of established points, attributed differences and open issues to carry into the conversation. DIGEST: summarize the actual record, preserving reservations and distinguishing claims from shared agreement.
- **Alternatives:** A neat summary may erase mixed priorities or hide an unresolved contradiction.
- **Disconfirmation:** A recorded dispute about an item, emphasis or supposed agreement requires a corrected summary. Unknown agreement remains unknown.
- **Stopping:** A sufficient summary ends review of settled points; preserve disagreement rather than manufacture consensus. Incomplete notes may require an explicitly qualified summary.
- **Source depth:** [INT-01](evidence-index.md#int-01) and [MI-02](evidence-index.md#mi-02) full guidance; [CRISIS-01](evidence-index.md#crisis-01)/02 full practitioner descriptions. No isolated empirical summary-effect estimate or scoring rubric adopted.

## MC-05: Pause, defer or make no inquiry

**Method baseline 1.1 · provisional; evidence baseline 2026-10-23.**

- **Uncertainty addressed:** Whether more inquiry would be useful, welcome or proportionate.
- **Context:** Reflection time, sensitive or irrelevant material, fatigue, explicit boundary, adequate existing evidence or no valid reason to ask.
- **Evidence needed:** Conversational context and expressed preferences; a pause alone cannot identify its cause.
- **Possible moves:** PREPARE: recommend space, deferral, a return to the practical task or no inquiry where useful; never suggest silence to pressure disclosure. DIGEST: respect recorded boundaries and avoid interpreting undocumented pauses or the cause of a silence.
- **Alternatives:** Silence can reflect thought, turn-taking, technical delay, distraction or discomfort; do not infer a trait from it.
- **Disconfirmation:** A recorded request for help, prompting or clarification can change the next recommended approach. A missing response or undocumented pause alone establishes no cause.
- **Stopping:** No duty to fill silence. A refusal, irrelevance or adequate answer stops recommended inquiry; do not turn DIGEST gaps into pressure for another conversation.
- **Source depth:** [INT-01](evidence-index.md#int-01) full methodology and [CRISIS-01](evidence-index.md#crisis-01)/02 full practitioner guidance. Evidence does not establish a universal pause duration or optimize silence for extraction.

## Interpreting feedback

Recorded correction is useful evidence, not failed rapport. In DIGEST, replace the mistaken interpretation, repair the summary’s emphasis and revise future practical recommendations. If repeated reflection feels formulaic or patronizing, stop it. A decline does not reduce a person's supposed trust score. Do not use validation to manufacture agreement or treat calmness as proof of truth.

LISTEN-01 supports reported understanding in a brief laboratory interaction, with nonsignificant satisfaction/attraction differences versus advice. LISTEN-02's behavioral associations varied across deep conversation and small talk. MI-02 and INT-01 are professional/methodological guidance; CRISIS-01/02 document a practitioner repertoire. None validates a universal listening score. Details and direct links: references/evidence-index.md.
