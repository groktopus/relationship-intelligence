# Designing inquiry and interpreting responses

Use this reference to connect a model gap to a useful upcoming conversation, and to connect a later answer back to that gap. It is a design discipline, not a bank of questions or a psychological test. Pair it with [person model](person-model.md) and the method family suited to the uncertainty.

## The inference chain

Make the chain inspectable:

**Existing evidence → competing explanations → consequential distinction → conversational opportunity → actual observation → scoped interpretation → model revision.**

A question's topic is not automatically the construct it measures. Asking about a changed deadline can reveal a stated constraint, an account of a decision or a relevant tradeoff. It does not, by itself, measure risk tolerance, locus of control or a stable personality dimension. A plausible story connecting an answer to a trait is a hypothesis requiring further evidence.

[MED-01](#med-01) supplies the construct-validity foundation: the interpretation of a measure requires a network of predictions and evidence, not a name attached to an item. That is a methodological argument, not empirical validation of this skill. Adapted wording, a new population, a different setting or a new scoring model changes what must be qualified.

## Prepare an inquiry packet

For a consequential gap, specify these components in the user's working preparation:

1. **Purpose:** what understanding could change and why it matters now.
2. **Known context:** actual shared history, prior answers, recent changes and the upcoming practical purpose.
3. **Alternatives:** two or more plausible accounts, including situational and mixed explanations where relevant.
4. **Natural anchor:** a real statement, event, decision or concern that makes the topic intelligible in this conversation. An anchor is context, not a fabricated pretext.
5. **Possible move:** a personalized question, reflection, comparison, explanation, practical option, summary or no inquiry. Select by method and context.
6. **Observation markers:** what the response could actually contain—explicit reasons, examples, criteria, priorities, qualifying circumstances, corrections or missing information.
7. **Interpretation branches:** which observations would support, weaken or fail to distinguish each explanation.
8. **Follow-up principle:** clarify the consequential distinction that emerges, using the person's own terms; do not prescribe a fixed sequence.
9. **Update consequence:** which model dimension and next guidance would change.

Keep the person-facing wording natural and compact. The reasoning can be richer than the question. A small number of well-chosen opportunities generally beats a comprehensive questionnaire for this workflow; this is a product design choice, not a quantified efficacy finding.

## Form the move adaptively

Start with the person's actual situation. Use an open invitation when their framing is unknown; use a focused comparison when the alternatives are meaningful and familiar. Reflect a tentative interpretation when checking understanding would be more useful than gathering more material. Offer concrete help when the answer is already clear. Refer to [focus and elicitation](focus-and-elicitation.md), [listening and pacing](listening-and-pacing.md), and [consumer-depth research](consumer-depth-research.md) for different forms of inquiry.

Avoid wording that builds the preferred explanation into the premise. A question assuming someone resists change cannot distinguish temporary workload, implementation concerns, disagreement with a proposal and a general preference. A relevant real episode can anchor exploration of what mattered in that decision. The response may reveal a mixture rather than selecting one account.

Examples in these references illustrate a distinction or reasoning move. Generate wording anew for the actual person and conversation; do not copy examples as routine scripts.

## Digest an evidence packet

Keep these fields conceptually distinct, using the host's own representation:

- Source and source-origin identity; event time, import time and available location.
- Modality: transcript, notes, email, audio, video or other material actually available.
- Speaker and attribution quality.
- Question as actually asked, including context; distinguish it from the planned wording.
- Response as recorded; exact spans versus paraphrase.
- Relevant surrounding exchange, qualifications, interruptions and corrections.
- The decision contract, if any, and what it can and cannot judge.

A written transcript supports words recorded and the exchange's structure. It does not establish unrecorded gaze, facial movement, vocal pitch, sincerity or physiological relaxation. An annotator's interpretation is attributed evidence, not a new directly observed behavior. Read [behavioral observation](behavioral-observation.md) when modality or demeanor matters.

## Rubric fit before scoring

Use a predefined response rubric when its intended observation matches the actual question, answer and context. Otherwise use qualitative interpretation or unknown. Record:

- Version and intended contextual construct or literal response feature.
- Applicable wording/context range and known exclusions.
- Labels, overlapping labels, mixed, unknown and none-of-the-above meanings.
- Numeric anchors, when applicable, stated in observable terms.
- Supporting and counterexample spans; negation and time scope.
- Validation evidence and its population, source modality and reference criterion.

For example, “the response explicitly names a prerequisite before proceeding” is a textual judgment. “This person has an external locus of control” is a different psychological interpretation needing different evidence. Agreement on the former cannot validate the latter.

A projective account about a hypothetical colleague first establishes how the speaker describes that scenario. It does not automatically establish the speaker's own behavior or trait. A word-frequency association from a long corpus does not license the same inference from one short reply. See [psycholinguistics](psycholinguistics.md) for empirical corpus conditions and calibration limits.

## Optional model and LLM parity

Both paths receive the same separated question/response/context packet and the same decision meanings. A shared batch may contain several independent textual judgments. Stage dependent judgments after their prerequisites. Preserve grounding per judgment and synthesize ambiguities at the main-agent level. Many judgments over one answer remain one source, not many corroborations.

Qualify each path against independent labeled cases. Compare false support, missed qualifiers, speaker errors, groundedness, appropriate abstention, latency and total cost. Numeric outputs are not assumed comparable across providers. Read [decision judgments](decision-judgments.md) and [evaluation](evaluation.md).

## Revision and disconfirmation

After DIGEST, state what changed: an account gained support, narrowed to one context, weakened, was overturned, or remains unresolved. Seek evidence that could distinguish the remaining alternatives in the next conversation. Do not keep asking a question already answered or treat every answer as confirmation. A useful model can become less certain when new evidence reveals a previously hidden tension.

## Sources and access depth

### MED-01

[Cronbach and Meehl (1955), Construct Validity in Psychological Tests](https://meehl.umn.edu/sites/meehl.umn.edu/files/files/036constructvalidityidx.pdf). Original methodological paper, full university-hosted PDF retrieved and read October 23, 2026 through GroktoCrawl. Explains construct interpretation, nomological relationships and evidence beyond criterion correlation; not an experimental study of conversational profiling or an endorsement of this architecture. Its historical classification of validity should not be mistaken for the whole current standards framework.

### MED-02

[NCME: Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing](https://ncme.org/resources/books/testing-standards/). Official institutional page accessed October 23, 2026; confirms joint AERA/APA/NCME provenance, 2014 edition and open-access availability. Only this page was inspected here, not the full book; no particular scoring protocol is attributed to the page.

Other method claims route to the source-level evidence records in the linked references and [evidence index](evidence-index.md). The inquiry packet is our proposed integration, not a validated instrument.
