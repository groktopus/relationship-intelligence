# Behavioral observation and non-reactive assessment

Version 1.0. Load when preparation or digestion depends on behavior, an alleged change from baseline, vocal/facial observations, environmental traces or forensic interviewing ideas. The useful output is a contextual hypothesis and a better discriminating conversation, not a decoding dictionary. Pair with [person model](person-model.md) and [context and revision](context-and-revision.md).

## What the evidence supports

Non-reactive evidence can reveal patterns that a direct self-description misses. It can also be ambiguous: an observable trace, the meaning an observer assigns to it, and its validity as a cue to a construct are separate links. Gosling and colleagues' two studies of offices and bedrooms compared independent impressions with occupant self/peer ratings and coded physical cues. They found some accuracy varying by trait and setting, alongside stereotype-mediated agreement. This supports examining cue validity; it does not make observer consensus a substitute for it. [BO-01](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/psp-823379.pdf)

The deception literature gives a particularly clear warning about overinterpreting behavior. A research review characterizes discovered nonverbal cues as weak and unreliable. A primary baseline experiment found promising differences under detailed coding but did not establish improved intuitive judgment from giving observers a comparable baseline. Microexpression findings depend on definitions and setting; an exploratory positive study coexists with a randomized training study that did not show improved lie detection. These are different research questions, not a single pooled conclusion. [BO-02](https://researchportal.port.ac.uk/en/publications/reading-lies-nonverbal-communication-and-deception/), [BO-03](https://pure.port.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/58209761/A_comparable_truth_baseline_improves_truth_lie_discrimination_FINAL.pdf), [BO-04](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02545/full), [BO-05](https://pure.hud.ac.uk/en/publications/a-test-of-the-micro-expressions-training-tool-does-it-improve-lie/)

Being relaxed does not establish truthfulness. A change in movement, speed, fluency, gaze or detail does not identify its cause. The plausible causal paths include topic difficulty, memory demands, stakes, familiarity, fatigue, language, setting and interaction with the interviewer. A behavioral description is often a useful place to start a question about context; it is insufficient on its own for a stable trait or veracity conclusion.

## Establish what the source actually contains

| Source | Potential observations | What is not available from it |
|---|---|---|
| Written notes | Attributed recollections and the note taker's interpretations | Exact wording, timing, tone or facial actions unless explicitly recorded; the original behavior remains second-hand |
| Plain transcript | Recorded words, speaker turns and explicit corrections, subject to transcription and diarization errors | Pitch, loudness, facial expression, gaze, gestures or measured response latency; punctuation is not an acoustic measurement |
| Time-aligned transcript | Words and supported turn timing | A timestamp gap can include recording/editing artifacts; it does not automatically establish silence, hesitation or cognitive load |
| Audio | Audible events and measurable vocal features where recording quality and speaker attribution permit | Visual behavior; emotion or motive is still an interpretation, not an acoustic feature |
| Video | Visible actions in the captured frame, with audio if present | Off-camera behavior or internal emotion; ordinary video may not support fine-duration facial coding |
| Existing work artifacts or environmental descriptions | Sequences of choices, revisions, organization and identity claims as actually represented | Who created/controlled each trace, why, or whether it reflects the person across contexts without further evidence |

In DIGEST, keep an observation record with source ID, event time, speaker/actor, modality, exact span or interval, observable event, measurement method and reliability. Mark a reported interpretation separately. A sentence such as “she seemed defensive” is the observer's interpretation unless the underlying words/actions are supplied. Do not silently turn it into a directly observed state.

If a source modality cannot support an observation, leave that field unavailable. The main language model and an optional decision model must use the same unavailable/unknown semantics. Neither can reconstruct a facial cue or voice from a transcript.

## Method BO-M1: contextual behavioral comparison

**Uncertainty addressed.** Is an observed difference a recurring pattern, a response to a particular context, a changed priority or an artifact of measurement?

**Suitable contexts.** Repeated conversations, prior work choices or existing artifacts with reasonably comparable circumstances. Especially useful when a current interpretation would change the next proposal or conversational approach.

**Evidence needed.** The observation itself; at least one comparison where available; event dates; topic, role, stakes, audience and task; changes in recording/coding; an account of who made the observation. Do not label casual opening talk a universal baseline for a consequential decision.

**Possible moves in PREPARE.** Identify the important contrast and formulate a natural question about what changed, which constraint mattered or what made one situation different. Anchor it to an actual shared event. Invite the person's account before offering an explanation. The private learning aim should distinguish hypotheses such as current capacity pressure, evidence preference, proposal-specific doubt and role-dependent responsibility. A practical comparison of options can be more informative than a question about personality.

**DIGEST interpretation.** Code what changed, what stayed comparable, the person's account and whether new evidence supports a situational or cross-context explanation. Keep observed choice separate from the reason offered and from inferred motivation. Confidence should reflect independent occasions and contextual coverage, not merely the number of coded cues.

**Alternative explanations.** Topic complexity, language proficiency, time pressure, different decision authority, prior conflict, a different interlocutor, fatigue, recording quality and an unobserved event. Apparent consistency can also reflect repeated circumstances rather than a broad disposition.

**Disconfirmation.** Find counterexamples in similar contexts; check whether the person corrects the premise; examine whether the pattern disappears when the presumed cause changes. If comparison quality is poor, narrow the proposition rather than inventing an explanation.

**Stopping.** Stop behavioral coding when no comparable source exists, the distinction would not change guidance, or the measurement cannot be supported. Retain the useful uncertainty for the next preparation.

**Source depth and transfer.** BO-01 supplies a cue-validity framework, not validation of transcript-based behavioral inference. BO-03 demonstrates why matching context matters and why successful research coding does not automatically transfer to intuitive judgment. This application to relationship learning is a design extrapolation.

## Baselines: compare like with like

A baseline is not a person's true inner state. It is a set of observations under specified conditions. Comparable topic, stakes, cognitive demands, role and source quality matter.

Bogaard and colleagues (2022) tested comparable truthful baselines in two experiments. Experiment 1 used 172 mostly student participants producing written mock-witness accounts; independent coders found different changes in detail between true and false accounts. Experiment 2 used 224 observers. Presenting baseline plus target did not improve accuracy over presenting targets drawn from the same baseline condition without the baseline. The authors explicitly regard practical baseline recommendations as premature. The distinction between coded group differences and improved individual judgments must survive into the skill's reasoning. [BO-03](https://pure.port.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/58209761/A_comparable_truth_baseline_improves_truth_lie_discrimination_FINAL.pdf)

For this skill, baseline mapping is therefore a **context map**: how this person has approached comparable situations, what is different now and what remains unexplained. It can improve questions and longitudinal hypotheses without purporting to establish truthfulness. A shift from expansive discussion to concise answers could motivate checking current priorities; it cannot by itself establish deception, disinterest or a stable communication trait.

## Paralinguistics: measurements are not meanings

Where audio exists, record a measured or clearly audible property before interpreting it: speech rate over a defined interval, turn latency, interruptions, a stated uncertainty or audible self-correction. State how it was measured and whether edits, overlap or recording loss affect it. Do not use textual commas, ellipses or automatic punctuation as a proxy for vocal stress.

A small within-person Italian study illustrates limits of generalization. Nineteen native Italian speakers supplied true and false opinions in recorded interviews. Average pitch did not differ significantly (d = .006), while response latency was longer in the deceptive condition (reported r = -.51) and speech rate slower (d = .447). This is a group finding from a specific laboratory paradigm, not a threshold for judging a new person's answer. The paper also discusses linguistic and cultural differences in cues and differences from earlier studies. [BO-06](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00453/full)

A useful relationship application is to notice that a particular topic took more explanation or repair and ask which distinction needs clarification. The evidence may support “this issue required more processing in this exchange.” Competing interpretations include novelty, complexity, distraction, language and careful consideration. It does not support “slow answer means concealment.”

## Microexpressions and facial interpretation

Keep three questions separate: whether a facial action occurred, whether it is associated with an emotional process, and whether it predicts deception or a stable person characteristic. Accurate recognition of a facial action does not complete the latter inferences.

Matsumoto and Hwang's exploratory study coded archival mock-security interviews from 77 community participants using expert facial coding. Negative expressions at durations ≤ .40 and ≤ .50 seconds differed between assigned groups (reported d = 1.21 and 1.02), but conventional .04–.20-second expressions were rare and did not significantly distinguish groups; ≤ .30-second results were also nonsignificant. Reported classification rates at the two broader cutoffs were 63.2% and 68.4% in this sample. The study varied duration cutoffs, combined negative emotions, and calls for larger preregistered replication. The authors disclose employment by a company selling related products. These details constrain the impressive-looking group effects. [BO-04](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02545/full)

Jordan and colleagues randomly assigned 90 participants to microexpression training, bogus training or no training. The primary abstract reports that training did not outperform either comparison and overall accuracy was slightly below chance. This is evidence about that training test, not proof that every facial observation is useless. Full methods were not retrieved in this review; no specific facial-scoring procedure is adopted from its abstract. [BO-05](https://pure.hud.ac.uk/en/publications/a-test-of-the-micro-expressions-training-tool-does-it-improve-lie/)

For PREPARE/DIGEST, a directly supported visible reaction can identify a moment worth understanding. Prefer the actual content and a contextual clarification over assigning the reaction a hidden cause. No facial-scoring rubric is qualified by this reference. In transcript-only mode facial observation is unavailable.

## Method BO-M2: evidence-focused narrative elicitation

**Uncertainty addressed.** What happened, in what sequence, what the person noticed and how they interpreted it, without allowing the questioner's hypothesis to supply the account.

**Suitable contexts.** Preparing to revisit a shared event, understand a decision episode or examine a discrepancy in existing accounts. The transferable element from forensic interviewing is disciplined elicitation and source separation, not an adversarial investigation.

**Evidence needed.** A clear learning purpose, the person's prior wording, any prior prompts and the actual account. During DIGEST retain enough of the preceding question to detect whether it introduced facts, restricted options or suggested an explanation.

**Possible moves in PREPARE.** Begin with an open invitation about the event or decision. Follow the person's own detail with a focused invitation to elaborate, then clarify necessary particulars. Make room for correction and “I don't know.” Prefer a temporal or decision sequence grounded in their account to an interviewer-supplied story. Illustrations teach question structure; generate fresh language from the relationship context.

**DIGEST interpretation.** Distinguish volunteered information from interviewer-introduced information, a self-reported reason from a reconstructed cause, and confidence from corroboration. Examine what the person explicitly corrected. Missing detail is a gap; it is not automatically evasion or lack of a value.

**Alternative explanations.** Memory limitations, a question interpreted differently, conversational relevance, an assumption introduced by the interviewer and incomplete notes. A fluent narrative may reflect rehearsal; a fragmented one may reflect ordinary recall demands.

**Disconfirmation.** Check the account against other dated evidence; ask how the proposed explanation would handle an inconsistent episode; preserve the person's correction. Do not count agreement with a supplied proposition as equally strong evidence to an independently generated account.

**Stopping.** Stop adding particulars when enough is known to change understanding or guidance; if successive prompts merely repeat an assumption, return to the unresolved gap rather than increasing confidence.

**Source depth and transfer.** The NICHD research review documents child investigative interviewing with field and laboratory foundations. It emphasizes open recall, later focused prompts and continuing interviewer feedback; field accuracy is often hard to establish because ground truth is unavailable. Adult relationship preparation is an extrapolation, not a validated use of the full child-forensic protocol. [BO-07](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2180422/)

## Rubric components and synthesis

Use response-level contracts whose categories are observable in the available source. Possible components include `specific decision criterion stated`, `alternative considered`, `constraint explicitly named`, `self-correction`, `interviewer-supplied premise` and `speaker attribution uncertain`. These components support interpretation; they are not a validated personality scale.

Each judgment needs a source span, question context, definition, counterexample and unknown condition. `No explicit criterion in this excerpt` differs from `person has no criterion`; `unclear attribution` differs from `none`. A timeline-change answer does not automatically measure risk tolerance or locus of control. Require evidence of the actual construct and competing explanations. If a decision model cannot meet the same evidence contract as the main language model, use qualitative interpretation or unknown.

Synthesize across independent events, not cue counts. A changed answer, slowed delivery and a tentative phrase in one response can share one cause and one source. Preserve their dependence. The next PREPARE should show what the observation changed in the person model and what question could resolve the remaining ambiguity.
