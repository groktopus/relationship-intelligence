# Context, competing explanations and revision

**Method baseline 1.1 · provisional. Evidence baseline: 2026-10-23, approved specification Revision 6.** These are evidence-informed behavioral contracts, not validated interventions or assessment instruments. IDs remain stable across revisions; preserve the version and evidence baseline with any rubric or evaluation using them. A source revision requires explicit review, not a silent change to a frozen evaluation.

## Purpose: an evolving understanding of the person and relationship

PREPARE and DIGEST serve a deeper, revisable evidence-grounded model of this person in this relationship: values and priorities, decision criteria, communication patterns, supported motivations and tensions, competing explanations, and consequential gaps. Use references/person-model.md for the model structure and safeguards ([person model](person-model.md)). “Digital twin” is an aspiration to useful contextual understanding, not literal replication or validated psychology. 

## Asynchronous use: PREPARE and DIGEST

**Product orientation 0.2.0: prepare the human before a conversation; digest notes or, preferably, a transcript afterward.** Adaptation happens across conversations. The agent does not monitor, manage or steer the live exchange. Conversational moves below are optional approaches for the human to consider, never instructions the agent executes on the other person.

In **PREPARE**, recall the authorized person/relationship model and identify a consequential uncertainty between competing hypotheses. Offer a small selection of personalized investigative approaches with reasons and a private account of what their possible answers could teach. A possible question or reflection must fit the history, vocabulary and purpose; avoid a script, required sequence or quota. The human chooses whether and how to use it, including taking practical action or making no inquiry.

In **DIGEST**, inspect what the supplied transcript or notes actually establish. Preparation suggestions are not evidence that a question was asked, an answer occurred, or agreement formed. Prefer a transcript for wording and sequence; notes may be selective recollections. Attribute uncertain speakers tentatively, preserve missing context and distinguish quoted words from paraphrase. Do not invent responses, motives, pauses or nonverbal behavior. Check interpretations against corrections and contrary evidence, then enrich, narrow or overturn the person/relationship hypotheses, revise the next preparation and propose any authorized memory change. A summary alone is not this model update. There is no presumed live feedback channel.

Load when a person-level interpretation would affect action, observations conflict, current circumstances differ from remembered ones, or guidance may be stale. Aim for a useful, corrigible understanding at the scope evidence warrants, not a comprehensive psychological profile.

## Keep observation and explanation apart

Distinguish observed behavior, the person's self-report, a third-party claim, the user's impression, a hypothesis and an unknown. Preserve whose account it is and when the event occurred. A brief response is an observation; lack of interest, a deadline, role constraints and a communication preference are competing explanations. Use several contextually relevant observations to distinguish a stable pattern from a situational response.

Compare alternatives that could materially change action. A discriminating clarification is worthwhile only if it is relevant and welcome; otherwise retain uncertainty and use a modest practical next step. Independent evidence is valuable, but repeated summaries of the same event are one source lineage. Several model judgments about one excerpt do not provide corroboration. Liking, flattering agreement and assumed similarity are not person-specific accuracy.

## Let changed evidence change guidance

Recall before recommending a next conversation. Compare the new claim with the earlier claim's time, context and scope. Coexisting priorities can explain apparent inconsistency; do not force a change narrative. A mistaken identity, late historical import or duplicate can also explain conflict. A correction should change both the current account and downstream guidance, not become an append-only note beneath an unchanged label.

Keep useful historical evidence appropriately attributed while superseding obsolete guidance. Do not silently erase a direct correction or user edit. If identity or authority is insufficient, propose clarification without merging or overwriting. Persistent updates require the actual host's authorized lifecycle and verification; load references/memory-lifecycle.md when performing them. Without persistence, give an explicitly unsaved current understanding and disclose the recall gap.

## Model depth comes from distinctions, not stronger labels

Represent a value or motivation as a supported contextual hypothesis, not an explanation for everything the person does. A stated priority, a repeatedly used decision criterion, an observed communication pattern and an inferred motivation have different evidential standing. Preserve that distinction. Ask what the relationship changes: the person may communicate differently with this user, under a particular role, or when a specific commitment is at stake. Do not flatten those differences into a universal profile.

Qualitative confidence should reflect evidence quality, independence, temporal relevance, context coverage and counterevidence. One clear self-report can establish that the person said something; it does not by itself establish a stable underlying motivation. Multiple independent, relevant observations may support a broader pattern, while contradictory contexts can require splitting or narrowing it. Use descriptions such as tentative, supported in this context or well-supported across the observed contexts, with reasons and scope. Do not fabricate percentages or treat agreement between model outputs as independent evidence.

Maintain competing hypotheses and meaningful gaps where they affect understanding or action. Include tensions when supported: a person can value both autonomy and coordination, or growth and stability. The conditions that change their relative importance may be especially informative. A new conversation may reveal which conditions change the balance. Preserve both values and the conditions under which each takes priority.

## DIGEST must change or explicitly retain the model

For each consequential new observation, identify which existing hypothesis it supports, weakens, narrows or leaves unresolved; consider a new hypothesis only where warranted. Preserve counterevidence and the best alternative explanation. If nothing changes, explain why the record is insufficient or merely repeats known evidence rather than manufacture novelty. Separate the observed answer from its interpretation and the guidance derived from it.

A changed decision criterion should alter the next preparation. A corrected motivation should remove advice based on the mistaken account. A context-specific pattern should prevent a global recommendation. Retain unresolved questions privately with their relevance and a proportionate possible route to learning, including no inquiry. Persistent changes follow references/memory-lifecycle.md; detailed hypothesis structure belongs in references/person-model.md, not in a second competing schema here.

## MC-06: Test contextual explanations

**Method baseline 1.1 · provisional; evidence baseline 2026-10-23.**

- **Uncertainty addressed:** Whether observed behavior reflects situation, role, relationship, enduring preference or missing evidence.
- **Context:** A consequential interpretation from sparse or apparently inconsistent observations.
- **Evidence needed:** Role constraints, event dates, opportunities and direct accounts; seek genuinely independent sources where available.
- **Possible moves:** PREPARE: compare plausible explanations and suggest an optional discriminating clarification only where relevant. DIGEST: compare actual contextual evidence and direct accounts, inspect contrary evidence, or retain unknown; distinguish the user’s impression from the other person’s account.
- **Alternatives:** Liking, assumed similarity, flattery and self-report agreement are different criteria from demonstrated prediction.
- **Disconfirmation:** New context, repeated contrary evidence or the person’s correction changes the explanation and next preparation. Insufficient speaker identity or selective notes can prevent a conclusion.
- **Stopping:** No discriminating evidence or legitimate need means no confident trait conclusion or further recommended inquiry. Keep uncertainty in the next preparation.
- **Source depth:** [ATTR-01](evidence-index.md#attr-01) and [PER-01](evidence-index.md#per-01) full original studies; [ASK-02](evidence-index.md#ask-02)/03 full conflicting analyses. These do not validate a general person-model accuracy score.

## MC-11: Revise relationship understanding over time

**Method baseline 1.1 · provisional; evidence baseline 2026-10-23.**

- **Uncertainty addressed:** Whether remembered priorities and guidance remain current.
- **Context:** New evidence, changing constraints, corrections or apparently contradictory history.
- **Evidence needed:** Canonical identity, source lineage, event/import dates, original wording and scope. Repeated copies are not independent evidence.
- **Possible moves:** PREPARE: recall current attributed understanding, including uncertainty and superseded guidance. DIGEST: selectively propose additions, contextual modification, contradiction or supersession from actual evidence; verify persistence only through authorized host tools when available.
- **Alternatives:** Context may differ rather than a person having changed; a mistaken identity or imported duplicate may create apparent inconsistency.
- **Disconfirmation:** A correction, source mismatch or stronger contextual evidence changes both the current understanding and next-conversation guidance; do not count preparation suggestions as corroboration.
- **Stopping:** Insufficient identity or evidence means no merge or overwrite. Missing persistence means disclose the gap; missing conversation evidence means retain uncertainty rather than invent an update.
- **Source depth:** [REL-01](evidence-index.md#rel-01) and [PER-01](evidence-index.md#per-01) inform contextual/longitudinal caution. The memory lifecycle is a proposed engineering requirement, not a validated psychological intervention.

## Synthetic application

An earlier account emphasized expansion; a later dated account emphasizes a constrained budget and service stability. Preserve both with their scope. Recommend a lower-burden option or no action if current constraints defeat the earlier proposal. Clarify priorities only if that changes a legitimate decision. Do not reinterpret the constraint as fear or treat an old preference as overriding the current statement.

ATTR-01 supports context-sensitive attribution in constrained experiments; PER-01 uses self-other agreement, not whole-person truth; REL-01 is correlational diary evidence. These motivate caution but do not validate the memory architecture or an accuracy score. Source links/depth: references/evidence-index.md.
